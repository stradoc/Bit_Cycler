### CPU and memory model

| Date | Decision | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2026-10-28 | **The memory hierarchy plan**: L1 → L2 → L3 → RAM page frames + HDD → SSD / NVMe, as a labeled miniature (16-byte L1, 256-byte RAM, 16-byte pages); cache levels play automatically, a page fault pauses the song for its own view. **Parked past the L1 since 2026-10-31** (only CPU and control-logic upgrades) | Covers the textbook's hierarchy without adding to the song's load; page faults are slow enough to pause for |
| 2026-10-29 | **RAM takes 60 ns** (approximate), so its cost in cycles grows with the clock (1 at 16 MHz, 240 at 4 GHz); **each cache level adds its own lookup time to a miss** (L1 ~1 ns; later L2 ~5, L3 ~10–15). Replaces ~80 ns | Patterson & Hennessy give 50–70 ns for DRAM; a full miss grows as the hierarchy is bought, while misses get rarer |
| 2026-10-29 | **A memory wait freezes the whole pipeline**, squeezed into 2 / 3 / 5 beats on a log scale with the real cycles counted; stores wait until the L1 brings a write buffer; no write allocate; instruction fetch is always fast | The textbook's in-order pipeline; squeezing keeps a 240-cycle wait from eating the song while every cycle still counts |
| 2026-10-28 | **One L1 shape**: 16 bytes, direct-mapped, 4 lines × 4 bytes (`tttt ii oo`), write-through with a write buffer; automatic in the song (no new keys), taught by the panel, the results and a briefing. Choosing a shape comes later | Tier 6 players already route, stall, guess and turn the dial; the textbook's first cache, where conflict misses show best |
| 2026-10-29 | **The L1 goes on sale once a trip to RAM takes over a cycle** at your rated clock; the memory wall briefing comes with the clock upgrade that crosses it, the cache briefing after buying the L1 | The cache only makes sense once you've felt the wall |
| 2026-10-29 | **The panel shows memory 0–31** with the L1 beside it; ADD ARRAYS puts its arrays a cache-size apart half the time | With 16 bytes of data in a 16-byte cache only first-use misses could happen; conflict misses are the clearest lesson for direct-mapped caches |

### A deep pipeline, with a trade-off (a6747ab)
- **Why** (team): "Don't branch instructions take a while usually?... I feel like the way it works right now branch prediction is kind of useless", then "players should be able to choose deeper pipelines or not, that would be interesting. They can unlock it, but it has a tradeoff." A wrong guess cost 1 cycle (the textbook's 5-stage pipeline, deciding branches in ID), so guessing barely showed. Explained in the session: the branch penalty is the pipeline's depth, not a trip to memory (an instruction-cache miss can hit any fetch, and loops stay cached).
- **The part** (`data/economy.ts` `DEEP_PIPELINE`; `sim/progress.ts` `deepActive`, `toggleDeep`, the shop's `deepItem`; `Progress.deepPipeline`, `'none'` for old saves): on sale after branch guessing on the 32-bit chip, $800, switched on as it arrives; Enter on its row switches it ON / OFF any time. Its line weighs it up for your hardware ("39 ns per instruction off, 36 ns on"). In use only where branches are guessed (tier 4 up, the 32-bit chip or wider); the CPU's name says DEEP PIPELINE.
- **What it does**: the rated clock x1.5 (capped at the power wall: at the top clock it gains nothing and keeps its costs, as real chips found), and a wrong guess costs 4 cycles (`PipelineOptions.wrongGuessCycles`): the instruction fetched behind the branch is flushed as before, then the front refills for 3 cycles with nothing reaching ID (`Pipeline.refillWait`, `refillCycles`). The refill holds the pipeline like a memory wait: what's ahead of the refetched instruction is only a bubble and the branch, which writes no register, so it costs exactly what bubbles would. During a memory wait the refill overlaps it. The board's estimates follow it (tier 4's default "not taken" makes jobs a little dearer in cycles; a 2-bit predictor comes out well ahead).
- **In the song**: the flushed note, then a short frozen refill drawn like a memory wait ("WRONG GUESS: THE DEEP PIPELINE REFILLS 2 / 3 CYCLES"), feedback "WRONG GUESS: 4 CYCLES LOST", a practice tip, a results row (REFILLING (DEEP PIPELINE)), and REFILLS in the stats. Retries and replays keep the CPU they started with (`SongSetup.deep`); `?deep` for testing. The run's frozen beats are `waitBeats` (was `memoryBeats`).
- **TICK's lesson** (`deep`, 8 steps, after buying it and in BRIEFS): ten smaller stages and a faster clock, the dearer wrong guess and the refill, the trade-off, the power wall, the switch in the shop; two new pictures (`deep`: 5 stages against 10 and where a branch is decided; `deeptrade`: SUM GRID's time both ways from the game's own model, guessing not taken, with a 2-bit predictor, and guessing right every time); then a practice with it on.
- **The developer screen**: a DEEP PIPELINE row (not bought / off / on); to fit it, CHIP WEAR went (`cheat.wear()` still does it), LESSONS and PLAY are one group, headings get a little more room (`ListMenu` `heading`), and the line about a row no longer repeats [LEFT/RIGHT]. `cheat.deep('on' | 'off' | 'none')`.
- Tests: 147 (`tests/deep.test.ts`: on sale and switching, the clock and the name, idle on a CPU that can't run it, the power wall, the board's estimates, old and edited saves; `tests/pipeline.test.ts`: two wrong guesses cost 2 x 3 extra cycles, all refilling, none with perfect guesses; `tests/pipelineRun.test.ts`: refills ignore presses and take 2 frozen beats each; `tests/songSetup.test.ts`: the setup's deep pipeline). No page errors.
- **Not checked:** how it feels at speed (the faster beat on top of the refills); real-browser behavior; the balance (price, 1.5x, 4 cycles) beyond the board's numbers. Not modeled: a deeper pipeline also lengthens data hazards (a load's value comes later), and the shop's 3D chip doesn't show the part.

### Lesson 1 takes a close look at every instruction (d4e13be)
- **Why** (team): "in the very first tutorial, can TICK go through and show the user the add instruction in depth? Like point to the registers and say 'See! Look, these two instructions got added and stored in this register'", then "Can you do it for each instruction?".
