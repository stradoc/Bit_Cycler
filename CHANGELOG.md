## 2026-10-30

### Real mechanical keys, a volume that feels right, a title splash with a start-up chime (uncommitted)
- **Keys are recordings of a real mechanical keyboard** (team asked for a free mechanical keyboard sound): ten single keystrokes (eight letters, Enter, Space) cut from "Typing on a keyboard" by SamsterBirdies, a Cherry MX Blue keyboard, CC0 (public domain), Freesound #489424, from its public preview. Cut with a short script: 684 keystrokes found, the 59 well isolated ones measured, eight typical letters and the heaviest (Enter) and deepest (Space) kept; 160-220 ms each, faded, level-matched, mono WAV (`game/assets/sounds/keys`, 168 KB; credited in `game/assets/sounds/CREDITS.txt`). `loadMenuSounds` decodes them when the boot or the menu opens; each press plays a random one a few percent higher or lower; the synthesized ticks stay as a fallback until they load.
- **The volume setting**: it did change every sound (measured: 0.83 / 0.47 / 0 peaks at 100 / 50 / 0%), but on a straight line, so it sounded as if nothing happened until near the bottom, and the menu's sounds were far too loud (a key peaked at 0.83). Now the gain is the setting squared (50% is about -12 dB, half as loud to the ear; measured 0.22 / 0.056 / 0.009 at 100 / 50 / 20%), and the menu bus's boost is 1.2 (was 2.5).
- **A title splash at the end of the boot**: the screen clears, BIT CYCLER builds up in big block letters a row at a time (the letter set gained B, L and Y) in a bright color of the era (green, cyan, white, teal), the tagline fades in under it, and a start-up chime plays (`startupChime`: our own, in the spirit of old operating systems' start-up sounds, not any real one's melody: a soft F major ninth pad swelling in, a rising arpeggio of bell tones C5-E5-G5-C6, a high shimmer, in a reverb built from decaying noise); it holds for most of the chime, then the menu. The menu's own arrival swell is gone (the chime covers it).
- Found while checking: headless Chrome throttles timers to ~100 ms, so an audio tap reading 43 ms at a time missed short clicks entirely (it read silence where the game was playing); measured with a 0.68 s window instead.
- Verified in headless Chrome: the keys load (no errors), the volume steps, the 8-bit splash (the tagline first overlapped the letters: placed by the title's size before it started empty), a full boot peaking 0.65 at most. Not captured: the DOS splash (same code, cyan).

### Menu sounds modeled on SUPERHOT's terminal kit; no modem (uncommitted)
- **The modem is gone** (team: too much): the dial-up steps, its sound and `MODEM_NUMBER`. The boot keeps its other sounds.
- **What SUPERHOT's computer uses** (looked at for ideas only; none of its audio is used): its clip names show a small terminal kit, three typing sounds rotated plus their own Enter and Space, a sound for every new line (three rotated), short `ping`, `pong`, `Error`, `IncomingMessage` and `beep` sounds, a 3 s start-up, a logo sound, a 2.3 s swell as the menu comes in, and a long ambient loop. The short ones are plain PCM, so we measured them: typing is a dry 160 ms tick (60% of its energy at 1-2.5 kHz, 27% at 2.5-6 kHz, almost no low end); Enter is about 4x louder and broadband; ping and pong are two quick taps and a 1 kHz tone with high sparkle; the error is a flat 70 ms buzz around 128 Hz; the message tick is tiny and very bright. Our earlier menu sounds were bright and busy, then soft and thumpy: both wrong for that.
- **Now** (`audio/menu.ts`): keys are dry ticks in three rotating pitches (1.18, 1.35, 1.52 kHz) with their own Enter and Space (`typeSound(scene, kind)`); opening something pings, going back pongs (a new `back` menu sound, also on leaving the settings, saves and developer screens); an error is a flat buzz (a little 128 Hz body, mostly its upper harmonics); every new line of the boot ticks (`lineSound`; the fast loading scroll every third line, softer), and so does each file as the menu lists itself; the POST beep is a short flat beep; the power button plays a ~3 s start-up (a low tone and soft noise rising, faint static crackles, a soft chime), and the menu arriving swells in (`menuSwellSound`). Measured on the game's own code against the references: the typing tick 58% / 28% (theirs 60% / 27%), Enter, Space and the line tick close too, the error's body and buzz split like theirs; the ping still has more middle than theirs. Peaks stay under 0.6 through a full boot.
- Also found for the team (not used, a decision for later): Kenney's interface sound packs are CC0 (public domain: free to ship in a public repo, no credit required).

### Menu sounds redone, after SUPERHOT's computer (uncommitted)
- **Why** (team: the menu's sounds felt bad; the game's are fine). We looked at SUPERHOT for inspiration only, nothing taken: its sound names point to an ambient noise controller and soft terminal sounds, and the one plain sound file its launcher ships (a terminal cursor sound) measures as 160 ms, mostly in the first 40 ms, with 22% of its energy in a low thump (100-125 Hz), 45% in a tick at 1-2.5 kHz (a tone near 1.2 kHz), and only 3% above 6 kHz. Ours was the opposite: the key's snap at 5.6 kHz with a square tick at 3.1 kHz, a ringing spring and two clicks (bright and busy), and a hum of six steady tones (55-270 Hz) beating into mud.
- **Now** (`audio/menu.ts`, rewritten): every menu sound goes through one soft bus (highs rolled off above 7 kHz, a gentle compressor, level brought up). A key is a low thump, a soft pitched tick and a little click texture, then a faint tick as it comes back up; measured on the game's own code, the light key is 29% thump and 56% tick, peaking about 0.1, like the reference. Opening something adds a short whoosh rising in pitch (a window sliding in) instead of two blips; an error is a light thump and two short muffled low buzzes. The hum is mostly soft noise breathing slowly (every twelve seconds or so) with one low tone (57 Hz), the CRT's faint whine and softer drive seeks; its level is about half what it was. The boot keeps its sounds, softened: a low "thoom" and a quieter degaussing hum at power-on, a softened 1 kHz POST beep (triangle, not square), softer floppy steps and head ticks, the modem's speaker turned down (0.5 -> 0.3).
- How it feels still needs a listen: the measurements say it's balanced like the reference, not that it's right.

### A dial-up modem, a calmer hum, block-letter text files, and a chip that turns cleanly (uncommitted)
- **The fan's blade chop is gone** (team), and the hum is calmer and darker, closer to the moody hum under SUPERHOT's computer: softer airflow, a quieter motor, a deep drone of two low tones a fraction apart (55 and 55.35 Hz) beating slowly, the drive's platters, a fainter bearing rattle, mains hum and the CRT whine (`playMenuMusic`). `menu.ts` had been damaged outside this session (two Ctrl+Z characters and about ten missing lines at the end of the hum, which no longer compiled); the team cleared the characters, and the missing lines came back with this rewrite.
- **A modem handshake in the boot** (team; `modemSound` in `audio/menu.ts`): the program dials the contract exchange (`DIALING THE CONTRACT EXCHANGE...`, `ATDT5550142`, then `CONNECT 1200 / 2400 / 28800` by era; 555-0100 to 0199 are numbers set aside for fiction). Built from the standard tones through a phone-line band (300-3400 Hz): off the hook, the dial tone (350 + 440 Hz), the number in touch tones, the 2100 Hz answer tone, the two sides negotiating (980 / 1180 Hz and 1650 / 1850 Hz, hopping 300 times a second), on the 28.8k modem the probing chords (1200 + 2400 Hz) and a sweep, then the training hiss until the speaker cuts off. - **Opening something in the menu** adds two soft rising blips after the key click.

### Memory wall and L1 cache (committed in 0ea9423)
- **RAM takes 60 ns at any clock** (`sim/memory.ts`): 1 cycle up to 16 MHz, 2 at 32 MHz, 60 at 1 GHz, 240 at 4 GHz. A load or store reaching MEMORY (or running on the single-cycle CPU) freezes the whole pipeline until it's done (`Pipeline.memoryWait`, `wait()`); the frozen cycles count in CPI and CPU time (`memoryCycles`). Instruction fetch is always fast.
- **In the song** (`rhythm/pipelineRun.ts`, `scenes/PipelineScene.ts`): a wait is squeezed into 2, 3 or 5 beats (`freezeBeats`; 1.5x the first 1 / 2 / 3 after playtest: it should hurt); presses are ignored; the notes hold still (they're drawn on a clock that stops during the wait); the load's note says WAITING ON RAM (or MISS -> RAM) while the request runs up the bus and the data comes back; the panel counts "137 / 239 CYCLES FROZEN"; the music drops to a low drone with fast ticks, then a bright double click when the data arrives. The note on the line after a wait takes any press. Heat takes one idle step per frozen beat (`Chip.waitBeat`). Replays are indexed by decisions (`run.steps`), not cycles.
- **L1 cache** (`Cache`, `MemorySystem`): 16 bytes, direct-mapped, 4 lines x 4 bytes (`tttt ii oo`). A hit fits in MEMORY; a miss checks L1 (1 ns) and fetches the block from RAM (61 ns). Stores are write-through with a write buffer (never wait, no write allocate). Misses are labeled first use / conflict / too small (against a same-size cache that could put blocks anywhere, least recently used out).
- **Panel**: PROGRAM shows 5 lines; MEMORY shows addresses 0-31 (two blocks per row); with the cache, an **L1: LINE TAG DATA** box beside it (a missed block fills its line when it arrives); a **lookup line** under them ("lb 5: 0000 01 01 LINE 1 TAG 0 -> HIT", or "lb 0 -> RAM: 60 ns = 240 CYCLES AT 4 GHz"); stats add WAITING ON RAM. Clicking a load or store in a replay explains its trip (hit, which kind of miss, cycles); clicking a cache line explains what it holds and which blocks share it.
- **Results**: a WAITING ON RAM (n TRIPS) row in "where the cycles went", the L1 hit rate and miss kinds, a NO CACHE (BEST) comparison, and every comparison now at the rated clock with RAM waits included (single-cycle at a quarter of the clock).
- **Shop and board** (`sim/progress.ts`, `data/economy.ts`, `flow.ts`): the L1 CACHE goes on sale once RAM takes over a cycle; clock upgrades show a typical job's time per instruction before and after ("17 ns -> 16 ns (5% less). A trip to RAM still takes 60 ns: 60 -> 120 cycles"); board estimates include RAM waits (and single-cycle CPUs too). New briefings: **THE MEMORY WALL** (4 pages: the trip, cycles per trip by clock, the floor chart from the game's own runs, how it plays) and **THE L1 CACHE** (7 pages: desk analogy, lines and blocks, the address split, locality, three kinds of miss, how it plays, the score). Chip art shows `[L1 CACHE 16B]`; `cheat.cache()`.
- **Generator**: programs may use addresses 0-31; ADD ARRAYS puts its second array at 8 or 16 (16 = conflict misses on every load). **x1 is always NEXT and x2 always END** (`registers()` in `sim/generator.ts`; the shuffle still runs, so each seed's data, layout and client are unchanged); a test checks all 3,000 seeds.

### Overheating stops the clock; a worn-out chip overheats at 100% (committed in f58e570)
- **An overheat stops the clock** (`sim/thermal.ts`, `rhythm/pipelineRun.ts`): instead of dropping to the rated clock, the chip runs no cycles until it cools to its resume point. Each beat with the clock stopped (`Chip.coolBeat`) only leaks a little power (`haltPower` 0.2), so it cools fast: 5-8 beats. Notes freeze in place (every due beat moves back a beat), key presses are ignored, the music stops, and the gauge says CLOCK STOPPED. The panel says "OVERHEATED: CLOCK STOPPED / COOLING 66°C -> 59°C" and the damage, then "COOLED: CLOCK RUNNING AGAIN". Replays show the same stops (they come from the model).
- **Time lost cooling counts:** stopped beats add real time (`haltedMicros`) but no cycles, so CPI is unchanged while the contract's pace includes them. The results add "CLOCK STOPPED TO COOL FOR 7 BEATS: NO CYCLES, BUT 109 ns LOST (+3.4 ns PER INSTRUCTION)".
- **Worn all the way down:** the overheat point keeps dropping to 65°C at a top clock of 100%, so a worn-out chip at its rated clock overheats in about 28 busy beats, like 150% on a new one. 90% only just stays safe and 80% easily; the fan still keeps any top clock safe. It can't wear past 100%.
- **Wear and the overclock cap are separate** (`Chip.canOverclock`, `dialCap`): without the overclocking upgrade, H stops at 100% but the chip keeps its own wear; practice always runs on a new chip, so it never overheats.
- Lesson cards (thermal throttling, heat wears the chip), the lesson chart (squares now mark stopped beats), and the glossary describe it.
- Tests (72): an overheat stops the clock, runs no cycles, cools in 3-12 beats and counts its time; a worn chip's top clock (down to 100%) overheats within a beat of 150% on a new one; a worn-out chip keeps stopping but always restarts and never wears past 100%; 90% and 80% are safe when worn out; no overclocking upgrade caps H at 100% and is safe on a new chip; in a song, a stopped clock ignores presses and pushes the next note back.
- Verified in headless Chrome: a tier 3 contract on a worn-out chip at 100% overheated at 65°C around cycle 40, stopped for 7 beats with the freeze message and gauge, ran again, and the results showed the cooling time.

### Overclocking, CPU temperature, chip wear, and fire (committed in e700c0d)
- **Clock dial in the song:** `H` raises the clock 10%, `G` lowers it, from **50% to 150%** of the rated clock (tiers 2-6; every song starts at 100%). One beat is one cycle, so the tempo follows from the next beat: overclocking speeds the song up, underclocking slows it down to give time to think, at the cost of more CPU time. Practice always runs at the rated clock. Replays reproduce the dial cycle by cycle.
- **Heat model** (`game/src/sim/thermal.ts`, all numbers in `THERMAL`): power = clock x voltage², with +4% voltage per +10% clock, so +50% clock = 2.2x power (the power wall). The chip warms toward 40°C + 30°C x power (70°C busy at rated, 89°C at 130%, 105°C at 150%) with a 25-cycle time constant; stall/bubble cycles use 70% power. Only 140% and up can overheat; at 150% it takes about 45 busy beats, with smoke and flames as warnings. At **95°C it throttles** to the rated clock (the song slows) until it cools to **80°C**. CPU time is summed cycle by cycle at whatever clock each ran.
- **Chip wear:** each overheat lowers the chip's maximum clock by 10% (150% -> 140% ...), never below rated. It's saved (`chipMaxDial`) until repaired: `X` on the title screen, free until the shop exists (then it will cost money).
